Monthly magazine

Issue → article → writer.

Use five main destinations: This month, Past issues, Books, Writers and Write. Home shows the current cover and three article previews; full contents live on one issue page. Past issues are grouped by year. Keep the editor’s note, About and Contact easy to find in the footer.

Remove duplicate destinations and stale notices from the reading paths. Preserve original writing and scans. Ebooks use a quiet book reader. Only source-compared transcription enters text mode; other pages retain the original scan while proofreading continues.

01 / Illustration language

One family of images. Many kinds of feeling.

Use the story’s emotional centre to choose the scene. Expressive cartoon drawing and flat painted colour hold the family together; the writing decides the mood, setting and viewpoint.

Make it feel inhabited

Children attend to the boat, animal, puzzle or person in front of them. Fabrics have weight, rain makes surfaces wet, and light comes from somewhere. A few expressive lines can say more than detail everywhere.

Fantasy is welcome when it belongs to the writing. Younger readers can enjoy expressive characters while older readers find atmosphere and narrative depth.

Keep the world varied

Follow the text into villages, towns, schools, contemporary homes and imagined places. Avoid turning every article into the same rural memory, festival scene or smiling group.

Do not add picturesque dirt, grain or worn surfaces to prove authenticity. Quiet areas, ordinary gestures and selective detail create a more convincing world.

Keep the existing magazine masthead. Do not tint its colours or redesign printed-book covers to match the palette.

Noto Serif Oriya for literary text; Noto Sans Oriya for navigation. Body: 20px with generous line spacing. Card titles: 21px. Bylines and controls: at least 14px in new templates.

Preserve Odia shaping and poem line breaks. Avoid forced justification, tight spacing and text over pictures.

03 / Page patterns

Discover, choose, read.

Edition contents

  1. Start with the edition name and date.
  2. Offer simple genre links, followed by grouped article cards.
  3. Each card contains one image, unchanged title, author and reading link.
  4. Use three columns on desktop, two on tablet and one on a phone.

Frame artwork at 3:2 with a quiet paper surround. Preserve the full composition; do not stretch or crop faces to force a match. Let longer titles wrap naturally.

Reading page

  1. Show the edition breadcrumb, genre, title and byline.
  2. Keep one supporting illustration near the opening, without a full-screen hero.
  3. Give the writing a comfortable single reading column.
  4. Finish with a clear return to the edition and existing feedback options.

Keep pages static. No moving backgrounds, autoplay, pop-ups, image parallax or decoration between every stanza. Use a small motif only when it supports the text.

Accessibility and image use

Keep text as real text. Give linked cards one clear accessible name and a visible keyboard focus. A decorative card image can use empty alternative text when its linked title provides the destination; a meaningful illustration on the reading page should have a concise scene description. Never rely on colour alone. Preserve image dimensions to reduce layout shifts and lazy-load images below the opening view.

Covers, portraits and existing material

Printed covers remain unchanged. A new cover may be commissioned for a confirmed digital-only publication; keep the title and authorship exact and preserve the original asset. A PDF or a scan alone does not establish digital-only status. Use genuine photographs for author identity, never an invented likeness. Retain original article artwork in the archive until a replacement is commissioned.

04 / Next edition

A repeatable editorial rhythm.

Read

Read the complete text. Record title, author, genre, setting, emotion and the passage supporting the visual idea.

Choose

Select one scene and viewpoint. Check the neighbouring articles so a whole edition does not repeat the same composition.

Illustrate

Use expressive cartoon characters and a clear narrative moment. Simplify faces, folds and scenery; avoid photographic rendering. Keep lettering out of the artwork.

Review

Look at normal card size first. Check story fit, gestures, anatomy, cultural details and emotional tone. Correct specific problems.

Place

Use the same image on the edition card and reading page. Keep all editorial wording intact. Check desktop and phone.

Record

Save the source reference, exact prompt, selected asset and review notes. Keep original assets and production records.

Start with a moment, not a style filter.

Continuity: [recurring character or setting references, when relevant]

Avoid: [specific unsupported elements, spoilers or visual problems]
Output: landscape 3:2, no lettering, title, author name, logo or page layout. Keep the emotional anchor legible at card size. Do not arrange every person or object to face the viewer.

Before publishing

Does this image belong to this story? Does its subject read at card size? Do gestures and objects make sense? Is the mood appropriate? Does the edition have variety? Are the title, author and writing intact?
