01 / Literary adaptation study

Gudul–Gudli

overview

The world & its purpose

Concept and origin

This set began with the characters in Snigdha Nayak’s Odia serial about Gudul and Gudli’s mysterious journey. The source establishes the children in Class V in Ajuba, their affection for the missing Elin, Gudul’s ability to understand animal language, and a shared investigation. The exact relationship between the two children is not settled in the recorded source material. [1]

Visual and emotional direction

The developed cast uses expressive faces, natural brown skin and warm interactions. Gudul’s selected presentation has an Aal Red shirt, cream lower garment and blue waistcloth. Gudli has two low plaits, a gold blouse and a blue skirt with a checker hem. These are adaptation design proposals; western, coastal and Habaspuri-inspired alternatives remain part of the archive. [2]

The strongest emotional thread is care expressed through action: listening, making room, trying something and correcting a small mistake. The new short stories explore this thread outside the unresolved serial chronology. They are separately developed project scripts, not stories credited to the serial’s author. [2, 3]

characters

Meet the four characters

The attentive investigator

Gudul

Gudul wants to understand what is happening and do something useful for his friends. In the source, he understands animal and bird language and shares the investigation with Gudli. His developed personality adds attentive listening, an earnest wish to help and a tendency to wait for certainty or make a plan too tidy too soon.

His humour comes when a careful explanation misses an ordinary practical detail. His growth is to ask a useful question, admit what he does not know and attempt the next sensible action. Keep his dialogue brief and curious. A listening tilt, open hand and thoughtful pause can express him; he also needs impatience, delight, fear and silliness. He should not become an all-knowing interpreter. [2]

Performance range
Move between a listening tilt, a careful explanation, impatience and an open invitation. Let the reader see him thinking before he speaks.
Useful tension
He can understand a message without immediately understanding what to do. Gudli’s practical response can expose a gap in his careful plan.
Continuity
Keep the selected red shirt, cream lower garment and blue waistcloth. Class V is established; an exact age and his relationship to Gudli are not.
Writing guardrail
Animal-language understanding comes from the serial. It must not turn him into someone who knows every answer, or establish new powers for Elin.

The practical problem-solver

Gudli

Gudli turns an idea into something the group can try. The source already gives her active problem-solving moments, including a toy-parrot substitution and questioning a light in an apparently empty house. The developed personality emphasizes practical imagination and a willingness to act.

Her first workable idea can feel complete before she notices its effect on someone else. A confident claim followed by a self-amused correction is a useful comic beat. She grows by noticing the consequence, owning the mistake and changing the plan. Her forward lean and ready hands should coexist with hesitation and tenderness. Neither child is permanently the leader or the one who is right. [2]

Performance range
Give her active hands and a forward lean when trying an idea, then a visible pause when she notices a consequence. Include tenderness and uncertainty.
Useful tension
She moves the scene forward; Gudul’s questions may make the first solution better. Her correction should feel like growth rather than a punishment.
Continuity
Use two low plaits, a gold blouse and the blue skirt with checker hem for this selected presentation. Other wardrobe studies are alternatives.
Writing guardrail
Her source actions support initiative. Additional habits and comic timing are project proposals. She is neither an assistant to Gudul nor permanently right.

The eager participant

The parrot

The source bird is described with green and yellow colouring, red markings, a crest and a colourful tail. It takes initiative in seeking information. Its developed role is a small participant whose contributions and physical needs matter. Pride can be interrupted by a sleepy blink, a stretch or an awkward perch. In the new short pilots, chirps and visible behaviour carry the performance. No proper name, exact species or sex has been assigned from the illustration. [1, 2]

Performance range
An upright proud pose, an awkward perch, a stretch and a sleepy blink provide distinct beats without a speech bubble.
Useful tension
A small body needs a real place in the composition. Its preferred perch or timing can disrupt a plan that seemed complete to the children.
Continuity
Preserve the green and yellow body colours, red markings, crest and colourful tail of the described source bird.
Writing guardrail
Do not infer a real species, personal name or sex from the image. Distinguish its source initiative from newly invented short-story behaviour.

The gently persistent companion

Elin

Elin is a pale dog-like companion with large round eyes and small wing-like features near the waist. The source leaves the origin and the function of those features unresolved. The proposed short-story personality is gently persistent: Elin approaches, tries, pauses and chooses whether to join. Gaze, ears, paws and posture carry the action. Elin should be allowed ordinary agency rather than always being carried or rescued; no new flight ability or translated thoughts are established. [1, 2]

Performance range
Let Elin approach, inspect, pause, accept or decline. Eyes, ears, paws and a final position change should carry the thought.
Useful tension
The children may believe they have helped before Elin has actually chosen a comfortable place. Leave room for that last independent action.
Continuity
Retain the pale dog-like form, large eyes and small waist features. Their exact anatomy and purpose remain unresolved.
Writing guardrail
Avoid confirming flight, origin, a rescuer identity or translated inner speech. The selected ensemble is a proposed moment outside serial chronology.

relationships

How the group works

Gudul ↔ Gudli
A question meets an attempt. Gudul can make room for uncertainty; Gudli can make a plan tangible. Either may be the first to notice a need or the one who changes course.
Children ↔ parrot
Listening involves more than understanding a message. A place to perch, enough space and the bird’s own initiative give it a role in the scene.
Children ↔ Elin
Affection should be visible through an invitation, patience or practical care. Elin’s choice to move or join completes the exchange.
Parrot ↔ Elin
Different speeds and resting habits provide visual contrast. The new short-story direction can use that contrast without assigning additional serial mythology.

stories

Stories & reading experience

Three short stories

The three-story collection develops small, self-contained situations with warm humour. Across the stories, the cast can share attention, notice an overlooked need or enjoy a game without resolving the larger serial mystery. The collection preserves 18 panels, 13 spoken lines and eight sound labels from the selected finished editions. [3]

StorySituation and dramatic purpose
Room for One MoreA limited dry space seems too small for everyone. Small changes in posture and position make room; the animals’ needs shape the solution.
Who Moved?A keep-still game becomes funny when the surroundings move and a resting companion unexpectedly wins. Attention and timing carry the scene.
Drip, Tap!A roof drip becomes a shared rhythm. The cast listens and joins in, then a quiet pause and a final bird call finish the joke.

What has been made

Character development. Construction, expression, colour, clothing and acting studies; selected individual illustrations; an ensemble picture; and a character bible distinguishing source facts from new personality proposals.

Story development. Scripts, staging studies, rough storyboards and finished pages for the three short stories. Selected revisions are preserved alongside earlier versions.

Reading editions. A local reading collection, offline HTML and ZIP editions, and a ten-page landscape PDF with contents navigation and embedded Odia lettering. These are digital reading editions. [3]

Role in the wider portfolio

This track documents the early literary and visual exploration. It is useful for discussing adaptation, character acting and the development of the short-story format. It should remain clearly labelled when shared alongside the newer original casts. Its source identities, distinctive animal designs and unresolved mythology are not the identity of Nilu–Chaiti.

The finished short scripts were developed within this project. Their credit should remain separate from the credit for Snigdha Nayak’s underlying characters and serial. The records do not establish author approval of the adaptation or a merchandise arrangement. This report records that distinction without reaching a legal conclusion. [1–3]

Remaining decisions

Any further adaptation work needs a defined creative scope: retain the short-story archive, develop another proposed episode, or explore a specific adaptation opportunity. The saved serial record does not settle later revelations, exact ages or kinship. These should not be filled in as facts simply because a drawing suggests an answer.

02 / Original story & activity range

Nilu–Chaiti

overview

The world & its purpose

Working Odia name: ନୀଳୁ–ଚଇତି. Nilu–Chaiti is the selected provisional name and may change. [4]

The concept

Nilu–Chaiti is an Odisha-connected world of observing, making, playing and helping. A thoughtful listener and a quick practical experimenter approach the same small problem differently. Their bird and puppy companions have needs and choices visible in ordinary animal behaviour. Neither child has an inherited animal-language power. [4]

What carried forward and what changed

The project retained its newly developed personality themes and three small story premises, then created fresh character appearances, drawings and staging. The new world does not carry forward Ajuba, the serial’s household mystery, the missing-Elin quest, Elin’s unusual anatomy or the distinctive crested parrot. The development record therefore describes more than a name change, while openly acknowledging the creative work that preceded it. [4]

The setting is broadly connected with Odisha through everyday life, Odia language and a palette informed by laterite, sea blue and ripe-paddy gold. No exact district, child age or kinship is fixed in the current brief. These remain available for later world development.

characters

Meet the four characters

The thoughtful listener

Nilu the thoughtful listener

Nilu wants to understand what is happening and help a friend in a concrete way. He observes carefully, listens and looks for patterns. His flaw is turning uncertainty into an overly tidy plan, or postponing an attempt until he feels sure. His growth is to ask a useful question and try a modest next step.

A compact square face, round glasses and short wavy hair make him easy to recognise. He wears a red short kurta with a blue pocket on his anatomical left, ivory cropped trousers and sandals. Use calm attention, a listening crouch and an open-hand invitation without limiting him to one serious expression. His humour is an earnest explanation meeting a small detail he missed. [4, 5]

Performance range
Begin with observation, then a question, then a modest attempt. Include surprise and silliness so his glasses do not become shorthand for always being serious.
Useful tension
He can overprepare while Chaiti has already started. His contribution becomes valuable when he names one specific detail they can test.
Continuity
Square face, round glasses, short wavy hair, red short kurta, blue pocket on his anatomical left, ivory cropped trousers and sandals.
Writing guardrail
No animal-language power. His helpfulness comes from observable evidence. An exact age, district and relationship to Chaiti have not been fixed.

The practical experimenter

Chaiti the practical experimenter

Chaiti wants to turn an interesting idea into something friends can try together. Her strength is practical imagination. Her first success can make her overlook a consequence, so her growth is to own a mistake, revise it and keep her curiosity. A pause after a confident claim lets her laugh at her own correction.

Her visual identity is an oval face and short wavy bob, with a blue A-line tunic, narrow gold neckline and muted-gold cropped trousers. Trying and rethinking are equally important poses. She must not become a permanently correct girl or Nilu’s assistant; either child can discover the answer, need help or make the final joke. [4, 5]

Performance range
Use a ready-to-try posture, delight at a first result, then a pause and a revised action. Let her laugh at her own confident prediction.
Useful tension
She can be right about the method but miss a companion’s need. Nilu may notice that detail, and she can be the one to improve the arrangement.
Continuity
Oval face, short wavy bob, blue A-line tunic with a narrow gold neckline, muted-gold cropped trousers.
Writing guardrail
Rotate who discovers, leads and needs help. Practical competence should not make her an infallible teacher or a constant helper to Nilu.

The eager bird

The eager bird

This companion is a small brown bird with a short tail and no crest. It is a new visual identity, with no fixed personal name in the current records. Alertness and initiative give it a reason to enter a scene; excitement can make a small discovery seem enormous. Proud intent, a sleepy blink and an inconvenient perch create gentle comedy. Keep its movement and body language birdlike. Its response is observable behaviour, not a supernatural translation device. [4, 5]

Performance range
Alert head turns, a brief hop, an oversized reaction to a tiny discovery and an inconveniently timed rest keep the performance readable.
Useful tension
The children’s idea of participation may differ from the bird’s preferred spot or pace. Its needs can change the shape of the solution.
Continuity
A small brown bird, short tail and no crest. Use selected model v2. A personal name and exact species remain open.
Writing guardrail
Body language and chirps communicate behaviour; humans may interpret cautiously but do not translate it as factual speech. Keep the animal ordinary.

The gentle puppy

The gentle puppy

The russet puppy has ordinary anatomy, a cream chest patch and no fixed personal name. It wants to join on its own terms. Patient attention and persistence are strengths; trying alone for a moment too long, or copying before understanding, create small difficulties. It can accept help while keeping the choice to approach, pause or move away. A serious inspection and a final paw adjustment provide its quiet comic rhythm. [4, 5]

Performance range
A serious inspection, a patient wait, a tentative approach and one last paw adjustment create a quiet comic rhythm.
Useful tension
It may try alone slightly too long or imitate a movement without understanding the game. Help works best as an invitation the puppy can accept.
Continuity
Russet coat, cream chest patch and ordinary puppy anatomy. Use selected model v2; a personal name has not yet been chosen.
Writing guardrail
Do not add Elin’s unusual features or history. Give the puppy an active choice in the ending rather than making it a prop to be rescued.

relationships

How the group works

Nilu ↔ Chaiti
Observation and experimentation are complementary. A story works when a careful question changes an attempt, or an attempt gives the observer something new to notice.
Children ↔ eager bird
The bird brings a small need or interruption into a human plan. The children adjust through attention and action, rather than through a magical conversation.
Children ↔ gentle puppy
Care leaves a choice open. A changed seat, accessible bowl or invitation can make participation possible without deciding the puppy’s response for it.
Bird ↔ puppy
A quick alert movement can contrast with a patient inspection. A tiny final animal action is often enough to finish the joke after the human dialogue ends.

03 / Regional family adventure

The Koraput cast

overview

The world & its purpose

The concept and home

Two cousins and their aunt turn everyday errands into small adventures near a fictional village and forest edge. The contemporary world includes school routes, a family courtyard, a weekly market, fields, hills and familiar paths. The working audience is children aged 6–10, with adult-supported reading for younger children. [9]

Koraput in southern Odisha replaced the earlier Sambalpur–Hirakud direction. Regional research informs the landscape and broad clothing references. The family, village and occupations are invented, and no particular Adivasi community or ceremonial identity has been selected. Cultural connection comes through language, relationships, work and place as well as clothing. [9, 13]

Why this set is distinct

The adult is a recurring participant with her own work, mistakes and humour. Kusum’s desire for a plan meets Mandar’s imagination, while Chiku’s movement changes what everyone notices. Cream, rust, maroon, green and ochre distinguish the palette. Four model sheets supply 32 studies of views, expressions and actions. [9, 10]

characters

Meet the four characters

Kusum · 10 · the organiser

Kusum

କୁସୁମ | Girl aged 10 | Working profile

Kusum wants real responsibility, such as remembering the market errands. She organises tasks, recalls familiar places and follows through. Her difficulty is assuming that a remembered place must still look the same; she can become impatient or interrupt. A confident announcement followed by a quiet adjustment gives her humour. Her growth is to change a plan and share the lead. Two plaits, a cream blouse with rust sleeve bands and a rust skirt with two pale hem bands anchor her design. In the pilot, she recognises the rock at the turning place. [9, 10]

Performance range
Count errands, point out a route, interrupt in confidence, then look again. A quiet correction can be funnier than an exaggerated failure.
Useful tension
Kusum remembers the place; Mandar notices a change. Both forms of attention are useful, and either cousin can lead another story.
Continuity
Two plaits; cream blouse with rust sleeve bands; rust skirt with two pale hem bands; flat sandals. Taller than Mandar in the group reference.
Writing guardrail
Age 10 and these personality details are working proposals. Responsibility should include real decisions without making her a substitute parent.

Mandar · 8 · the imaginative observer

Mandar

ମନ୍ଦାର | Boy aged 8 | Working profile

Mandar wants a useful part in the outing and a good story to tell afterwards. He invents explanations, notices unexpected movement and lightens a tense moment. He can love an exciting explanation before checking it. His growth is to look again while keeping his imagination. He acts out sounds and movements, and can laugh at his own dramatic theory. Keep his short tousled hair, green shirt, ochre shorts and cream cloth on his anatomical left shoulder. In the pilot, he spots the path and supplies the hide-and-seek joke. [9, 10]

Performance range
Act out a theory with his body, pause to inspect a clue, and return with a grin. Wonder and concentration belong in the same character.
Useful tension
His explanation can be wrong while his observation is useful. Reba can join the checking process rather than simply dismissing his imagination.
Continuity
Short tousled hair, green shirt, ochre shorts, sandals and cream cloth on his anatomical left shoulder, even when the drawing reverses direction.
Writing guardrail
Age 8 is proposed. He is not permanently the foolish younger child; the pilot already gives him the key observation and final joke.

Reba Mausi · 31 · their aunt

Reba Mausi

ରେବା ମାଉସୀ | Woman aged 31 | Working profile

Reba sews and repairs clothes and cloth bags. She wants to finish her work, keep her promises and enjoy time with the children. She is practical and patient, yet can underestimate an errand or hurry past a detail. A dry reply can deflate a grand theory without belittling a child. She can forget something and accept a reminder. Her model uses a low bun, cream sari with maroon border, pallu on the left shoulder, work bag on the right and one right-wrist bangle. In the pilot, she selects another familiar route. [9, 10]

Performance range
Show her doing work, checking a bag, being tired, amused, uncertain and occasionally caught out. Her dry replies should preserve the children’s dignity.
Useful tension
She wants to finish the errand; the cousins want to investigate. A satisfying scene makes room for both purposes rather than treating hers as irrelevant.
Continuity
Low bun; cream sari and maroon border; pallu left; one work bag right; one bangle on the right wrist. Use the corrected v2 model.
Writing guardrail
Age 31 and sewing work are invented for this family. Let the children remind or help her. She does not supply every explanation.

Chiku · a free-living neighbour

Chiku

ଚିକୁ | Three-striped palm squirrel | Working nickname

Chiku is a free-living neighbour, not a pet or speaking guide. The children recognise the squirrel, which has its own activities and can come and go. Its movement brings distraction, visual comedy and occasional clues. The model retains three pale back stripes, a cream underside, small rounded ears and a bushy tail. Age and sex are unspecified. In the pilot, crossing a branch draws Mandar’s attention to the hidden path; helping is not presented as the squirrel’s intention. [9–11]

Performance range
Run, pause, look back and disappear behind a trunk. The reader can sometimes spot Chiku before the characters do.
Useful tension
The family’s attention follows a squirrel doing its own thing. A clue can be revealed accidentally, without an intention to help.
Continuity
Three pale longitudinal back stripes, cream underside, small rounded ears and bushy tail. Keep squirrel scale relative to the people.
Writing guardrail
A free-living three-striped palm squirrel, not a pet or speaking guide. Age and sex are unspecified. It need not appear in every scene or errand.

The proposed family
Kusum’s mother, Mandar’s mother and Reba are three sisters. Reba is therefore Mausi to both cousins. Nearby homes allow regular visits without requiring one household.
Kusum ↔ Mandar
A remembered plan meets an unexpected possibility. Avoid repeating an older-child/right, younger-child/wrong formula; each can supply a different part of the answer.
Children ↔ Reba

Family ↔ Chiku

stories

Stories & reading experience

ବର୍ଷା ପରର ବାଟ

After rain, a market errand becomes puzzling when a familiar path appears to stop. The family works out what has changed and chooses another route. The ending turns the apparent mystery into Mandar’s joke about a path playing hide-and-seek. [11]

How the story works

Reba’s need for sewing thread gives the outing a purpose. Kusum first claims to know the way, then doubts it, then recognises the rock. Mandar follows Chiku’s movement and sees the path behind the leaves. Reba chooses the alternative route. Each human contributes to solving the immediate problem; the squirrel remains an independent animal.

The finished digital v1 has separate Odia and English pages, a bilingual reader, shareable page images and an offline pack. The last two panels clarify the open left fork around the rock. The original script wording was retained. The rough storyboard and earlier art remain as revision history. [11]

1 · An errand with a purpose
Reba needs sewing thread. Kusum is confident she knows the way. The family’s outing matters before the puzzle begins.
2–3 · Doubt, then recognition
A fallen branch hides the familiar bend. Kusum recognises the marked rock, establishing that they have reached the right place.
4–5 · Observation, then a decision
Chiku crosses the branch; Mandar sees the path through the leaves. Reba chooses the open left fork. Each human contributes a different part of the solution.
6 · A shared joke
Mandar says the path was playing hide-and-seek. Kusum responds to the joke as they continue. The squirrel remains behind, engaged in its own world.

design

Visual direction & language

Four recurring places
The family courtyard starts and ends outings; the lane and school route support everyday encounters; the weekly market gives errands a purpose; the familiar forest-edge path introduces visible changes. These are invented places within the chosen regional frame.
Visual identity
Cream, rust, maroon, green and ochre; confident outlines and restrained flat shadows. Reba is tallest, Kusum taller than Mandar, and Chiku stays small. Separate model sheets are enlarged for study and do not establish a shared scale.
Language and relationships
Odia leads the story, with a natural English companion. Introduce Reba as the children’s aunt in English, then retain Mausi in speech. Use comfortable separate lettering layouts. Do not claim a local dialect from general Odia wording.
Regional specificity
Koraput is in southern Odisha, replacing the earlier western Odisha direction. The group is contemporary and fictional with broad regional clothing references. No particular Adivasi community or ceremonial identity has been selected; work, family and place carry the connection too.
