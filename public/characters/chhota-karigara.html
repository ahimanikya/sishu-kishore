The Young Makers

Tara sees what a thing could become. Kanu tests what it actually does. Tara’s artisan uncle Dinu brings experience, patience and the occasional habit of helping too quickly. Their workshop adventures turn mistakes into shared discoveries.

The world & its sources

Make, test, notice and try again

Setting
A fictional contemporary settlement near Konark; the workshop is not a reconstruction of the temple’s construction.
Family and friendship
Twelve-year-old Tara, her thirty-nine-year-old maternal uncle Dinu, and ten-year-old neighbour Kanu.
Relationship to tradition
Dharmapada is a traditional legendary reference for youthful craft and insight. Tara, Dinu and Kanu have their own contemporary lives and original plots. They do not depict Dharmapada or Bisu Maharana, and this series does not rewrite the legend’s ending.
Audience
Working audience: ages 7–11, with adult read-aloud appeal. The cast ages and names remain editable development choices.

Meet the cast

The maker · Age 12

Tara ତାରା

the young maker; Dinu's niece and Kanu's friend.

Wants
to make something useful enough for someone else to depend on.
Strength
thinks in shapes, notices alignment and can explain an idea with a quick drawing or model.
Flaw & vulnerability
becomes attached to a beautiful first version and skips the dull test that could reveal its weakness. mistakes a failed prototype for evidence that she has no talent.
Growth
learns that showing an unfinished attempt is part of working well.
Humour & habit
has an exact drawing for everything except where she left the pencil. turns an object slowly in her hands before deciding what is wrong.
Voice
precise when describing shapes, impatient when defending an idea, warmly excited when somebody notices an improvement she has made.
Design direction
tall, angular child silhouette; short practical bob tucked behind one ear; plain kurta, straight trousers and sandals. Soft cloth apron at the worktable only. The apron and drawing board create a recognisable pose.
Writing & drawing notes
magical genius who never practises, miniature royal costume, or a costume presented as a verified historical artisan's garment.

The mentor · Age 39

Dinu Mamu ଦିନୁ ମାମୁ

Tara's mother's younger brother; a sculptor with a small workshop who also enjoys making simple models. This occupation and family are invented.

Wants
to do dependable work and help the children enjoy the patience behind it.
Strength
understands materials, spots where a test went wrong and can turn a complicated process into a manageable demonstration.
Flaw & vulnerability
sometimes solves the problem for the children before they can try. wants to protect them from disappointment because he remembers his own difficult early attempts.
Growth
learns when to offer a question, when to wait and when to step in.
Humour & habit
gives a long explanation and then finds that Kanu has demonstrated the same point with a bottle cap and two scraps of paper. checks a surface with his palm, then looks again from another angle.
Voice
practical and patient, with room for surprise and self-correction.
Design direction
sturdy, grounded silhouette; wavy short hair and a modest moustache; cotton work shirt, loose trousers, sandals and a cloth at his waist.
Writing & drawing notes
making him a sacred authority or only a dispenser of advice. His own work, deadlines and mistakes remain visible.

The tester · Age 10

Kanu କାନୁ

neighbour and friend; the tester who asks what happens in actual use.

Wants
a useful part in the project, even when drawing is not his strength.
Strength
notices wobble, weight, reach and other practical details. Can repeat a test in the same way and remember what changed.
Flaw & vulnerability
calls an entire idea useless after its first spectacular failure. worries that the maker gets the credit while the tester is only the person who is allowed to break things.
Growth
learns to describe a failure precisely and recognise what already works.
Humour & habit
supplies an unexpected but practical test that punctures a grand claim. crouches to bring his eyes level with a wheel or work surface.
Voice
short, concrete observations; pleasure in a well-timed sound effect.
Design direction
shorter, rounder silhouette than Tara; cropped hair; small checked shirt, rolled trousers and sandals. A paper measurement strip can serve the pilot; do not give him a permanent heap of unrelated gadgets.
Writing & drawing notes
comic fool, destructive sidekick or a child who exists only to praise Tara.

How they work together

Tara and Kanu
An attractive idea and a practical test have equal value. Tara can revise a design; Kanu can describe what fails without dismissing the whole project.
Dinu and the children
Dinu offers a slower test, waits while they find the problem, and handles the tool when a new hole is needed.
Failure is visible
The old wheel is set aside, the axle is temporarily bare, and a replacement is fitted. The story does not make a hole move by magic.

The Wobbly Wheel

Tara’s toy cart bounces on a flat board. Smoothing the wheel’s edge does not help. A slow test reveals an off-centre axle hole. The children mark a new wheel, Dinu makes the hole, and the repaired cart carries two blocks. Their next test will add one block at a time.

Visual direction & language

Palette
Mustard and charcoal for Tara; faded blue and stone grey for Dinu; rust checks and muted blue for Kanu.
Drawing approach
Simple expressive cartoons, warm skin tones, readable silhouettes and restrained background detail. The cast concept establishes relative scale; individual model-sheet panels are not a shared scale chart.
Language
Odia leads the script and reader. English carries the same actions and humour naturally. Named speakers and separate editable lettering keep both versions readable.
Review status
Fifteen paired title, caption and dialogue entries per pilot have been compared in assistant review. Independent fluent Odia review remains pending; the current language does not claim dialect authentication.

What changed along the way

The pencil concept established three silhouettes and the workshop relationship. The colour pass simplified Tara’s kurta and Dinu’s waist cloth. Three individual model sheets fixed views, expressions and actions before the wheel story was staged and lettered.

More story possibilities

Editorial proposals for future episodes. These are not completed scripts or illustrations.

The bridge that bends
A small model bridge holds an empty cup, then changes shape when water is added.
A handle for two hands
A useful carrying handle proves awkward for someone smaller; the group revises who the object is designed for.
The pattern that does not meet
A repeating border looks right until its last unit. Kanu’s counting helps Tara adjust the spacing.
