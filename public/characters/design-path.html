Gudul–Gudli · design path

Room for One More · From the saved studies to the current digital proof.

Underlying characters: Snigdha Nayak’s Gudul–Gudli serial. The pilot script and these development illustrations are new project explorations, outside the serial chronology.

    Brief & story

    The example is Room for One More. The paired script records panel actions and the dialogue that the illustrations need to carry.

    Read the bilingual script
  2. 02

    Early visual direction

    An early colour study explores face construction, a listening tilt and a smile. Its blue top is an earlier wardrobe direction; the selected presentation uses red. No separate hand-drawn pencil original is recorded for this example.

  3. 03

    Selected character models

    Selected files: Gudul v2, Gudli v2, parrot v1 and Elin v1. Keep source-established details and newly proposed behaviour distinguishable.

  4. 04

    Rough storyboard

    The six-panel board follows the blocked approach, Gudli’s recognition, the open space and the parrot’s tail. A separate staging study tested the two movement problems before they were integrated.

  5. 05

    Finished colour art

    The selected colour art keeps the six story beats and leaves lettering separate. Compare the children’s expressions, Elin’s position and the tail’s move into shelter.

  6. 06

    Lettering & digital proof

    The collection brings all three short stories into a linked reading edition. Its first story spans pages 3–5. This is a digital reading proof.

Nilu–Chaiti · design path

Room for One More · From the saved studies to the current digital proof.

Original-world cast development using the project’s retained general story premise, newly staged artwork and dialogue. Development illustrations; working names.

  1. 01

    Brief & story

    The example is Room for One More. The paired script records panel actions and the dialogue that the illustrations need to carry.

    Read the bilingual script
  2. 02

    Early visual direction

    The new cast establishes the listener, experimenter, ordinary brown bird and russet puppy. Nilu–Chaiti are working names. The later model sheets make the identities repeatable.

  3. 03

    Selected character models

    All four selected model sheets are version 2. Their views, expressions and action poses fix recurring features before story and merchandise artwork is developed.

  4. 04

    Rough storyboard

    Six pencil-style frames test the wet paws, movement of the mat, the bird’s stool and the foot-as-pillow ending. This is a digital drawing treatment, not a scan of a hand-drawn original.

  5. 05

    Finished colour art

    The first colour version moved the stool to the other side in the ending. Version 2 keeps the bird and stool on the left in panels 5 and 6, preserving the puppy’s chin on Nilu’s sandal.

  6. 06

    Lettering & digital proof

    The six panels are arranged with editable Odia and English captions, then exported to a one-page PDF. The colour sheet is 1254 × 1254 px; the prior layout review records about 164 ppi for panel art at A4. It is not a 300 ppi production master.

    Bilingual story proof · PDF
  7. 07

    Printer conversion & physical sample

    Deferred. Once the printer’s specifications are available, check final size, image resolution, bleed, safe margins, colour profile, PDF standard, paper and finishing. A physical sample will establish colour, legibility, trimming and feel. That stage has not yet been completed.

What changed along the way

Pencil-style rough v1 → initial colour v1 → corrected colour v2 → bilingual page v1. The original colour file is included in the archive so the stool correction can be compared directly.

The Koraput cast · design path

The Path After Rain · From the saved studies to the current digital proof.

Original fictional family and development illustrations. Koraput is the regional frame; no named community or dialect authenticity is claimed.

  1. 01

    Brief & story

    The example is The Path After Rain. The paired script records panel actions and the dialogue that the illustrations need to carry.

    Read the bilingual script
  2. 02

    Early visual direction

    The ensemble places the cousins, their aunt and a free-living squirrel near a fictional Koraput village. It establishes the group’s relative scale and colour direction. The saved early stages are colour concepts; no separate pencil original is recorded.

  3. 03

    Selected character models

    Selected sheets: Kusum v1, Mandar v1, Reba v2 and Chiku v1. Reba’s corrected sheet clarifies the bag; the sari pallu remains left and the work bag right.

  4. 04

    Rough storyboard

    The board establishes the market errand, familiar rock, fallen branch and Chiku’s movement. It provides the composition to refine rather than a separate pencil-and-ink pass.

  5. 05

    Finished colour art

    The final edit clarifies panels 5 and 6: the open left fork curves around the rock, and the family follows it while Chiku remains at the branch. The first four story beats and script wording are retained.

  6. 06

    Lettering & digital proof

    The finished pilot has separate Odia and English lettering, with captions and named speakers. The PNGs are flat digital page exports. The merchandise PDF separately checks the bookmark and sticker at their proposed sizes.

    Odia lettered page · PNGEnglish lettered page · PNG
  7. 07

    Printer conversion & physical sample

    Deferred. Once the printer’s specifications are available, check final size, image resolution, bleed, safe margins, colour profile, PDF standard, paper and finishing. A physical sample will establish colour, legibility, trimming and feel. That stage has not yet been completed.

What changed along the way

Family concept v2 → four model sheets, including Reba’s correction → storyboard v2 → clearer route in finished art v1 → separate Odia and English page exports. Earlier Reba and storyboard files are preserved in the archive.
