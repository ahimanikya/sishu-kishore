The world & its sources

Questions, journeys and shared stories

Setting
A fictional contemporary Odisha town and neighbouring villages; no exact district is assigned.
Family and friendship
An eleven-year-old nephew and his fifty-two-year-old paternal uncle, a former library assistant.
Relationship to tradition
Abolakara supplies the broad relationship between a questioning companion and a storyteller. These names, family relationship, contemporary setting, appearances and scripts are new project work. No particular Manoj Das or Mohanty story is being relabelled.
Audience
Working audience: ages 7–11, with adult read-aloud appeal. The cast ages and names remain editable development choices.

Meet the cast

The questioner · Age 11

Benu ବେଣୁ

the questioner; Madhab's nephew.

Wants
to be included in the grown-ups' conversation and trusted to find an answer himself.
Strength
notices contradictions between an explanation and what is actually in front of him. Remembers a small detail that everyone else has skipped.
Flaw & vulnerability
wants the answer so quickly that he interrupts the person who could help. Sometimes enjoys catching somebody out more than understanding them. hides embarrassment behind one more question.
Growth
learns to ask with care, listen to the whole answer and accept that some questions need time. His curiosity stays intact.
Humour & habit
takes a sweeping statement literally, then discovers that his own neat explanation has an equally inconvenient exception. starts a question with one raised finger, then raises another because he has thought of a second question before the first has been answered.
Voice
quick, direct and specific. He can apologise without losing his spark.
Design direction
compact, wiry silhouette; rounded triangular face; short side-swept hair with a small stubborn tuft; expressive eyebrows; simple loose shirt, knee-length shorts and sandals. A small notebook fits in his pocket.
Writing & drawing notes
making every expression a knowing smirk; glasses and red kurta would also make him too close to the existing listener design.

The storyteller · Age 52

Madhab Kaka ମାଧବ କକା

storyteller, former library assistant, and Benu's father's younger brother. His occupation and family relationship are creative proposals.

Wants
to keep the pleasure of shared storytelling alive and make his nephew feel welcome in it.
Strength
remembers people, listens kindly and can make a crowded courtyard fall quiet at just the right moment in a story.
Flaw & vulnerability
reaches for a familiar story before checking whether it fits the real situation. Occasionally pretends he remembers an ending better than he does. worries that an admission of uncertainty will disappoint his listeners; Benu helps him discover that it can invite them in.
Growth
becomes comfortable saying, "I don't know yet. Let's find out."
Humour & habit
theatrical pauses, a dry reply, and the moment his own grand gesture is interrupted by an ordinary inconvenience. taps a folded umbrella lightly against his sandal while considering an answer; he never uses it to threaten or discipline the child.
Voice
warm, measured and playful, capable of a short unadorned admission.
Design direction
tall, long-limbed and upright; broad friendly nose; short salt-and-pepper hair and a small moustache. Rolled-sleeve cotton kurta, simply wrapped dhoti and practical sandals. Folded umbrella and cloth book bag.
Writing & drawing notes
a guru costume, a ritual identity inferred from clothing, an all-knowing expression or a frail stoop. His clothes are proposed everyday choices.

How they work together

Benu and Madhab
Benu’s speed meets Madhab’s dramatic pauses. Each can expose a gap in the other’s explanation without losing affection.
The audience matters
The listening children supply the two possible endings. They participate in the discovery; the adult does not own every answer.
A small continuing promise
The duo will ask the lender for the original ending. A later episode can return to that visit without claiming the imagined endings are the book’s text.

The Two Endings

A borrowed book has lost its last page. Madhab begins to invent an answer, then admits he does not remember. Two listeners imagine different endings, and Benu records them honestly as their own versions. The unanswered question becomes a reason to return to the person who lent the book.

Visual direction & language

Palette
Leaf green, warm brown, cream, dusty indigo and muted brick red.
Drawing approach
Simple expressive cartoons, warm skin tones, readable silhouettes and restrained background detail. The cast concept establishes relative scale; individual model-sheet panels are not a shared scale chart.
Language
Odia leads the script and reader. English carries the same actions and humour naturally. Named speakers and separate editable lettering keep both versions readable.
Review status
Fifteen paired title, caption and dialogue entries per pilot have been compared in assistant review. Independent fluent Odia review remains pending; the current language does not claim dialect authentication.
