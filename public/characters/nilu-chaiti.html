overview

The world & its purpose

Working Odia name: ନୀଳୁ–ଚଇତି. Nilu–Chaiti is the selected provisional name and may change. [4]

The concept

Nilu–Chaiti is an Odisha-connected world of observing, making, playing and helping. A thoughtful listener and a quick practical experimenter approach the same small problem differently. Their bird and puppy companions have needs and choices visible in ordinary animal behaviour. Neither child has an inherited animal-language power. [4]

What carried forward and what changed

The project retained its newly developed personality themes and three small story premises, then created fresh character appearances, drawings and staging. The new world does not carry forward Ajuba, the serial’s household mystery, the missing-Elin quest, Elin’s unusual anatomy or the distinctive crested parrot. The development record therefore describes more than a name change, while openly acknowledging the creative work that preceded it. [4]

The setting is broadly connected with Odisha through everyday life, Odia language and a palette informed by laterite, sea blue and ripe-paddy gold. No exact district, child age or kinship is fixed in the current brief. These remain available for later world development.

characters

Meet the four characters

The thoughtful listener

Nilu the thoughtful listener

Nilu wants to understand what is happening and help a friend in a concrete way. He observes carefully, listens and looks for patterns. His flaw is turning uncertainty into an overly tidy plan, or postponing an attempt until he feels sure. His growth is to ask a useful question and try a modest next step.

A compact square face, round glasses and short wavy hair make him easy to recognise. He wears a red short kurta with a blue pocket on his anatomical left, ivory cropped trousers and sandals. Use calm attention, a listening crouch and an open-hand invitation without limiting him to one serious expression. His humour is an earnest explanation meeting a small detail he missed. [4, 5]

Performance range
Begin with observation, then a question, then a modest attempt. Include surprise and silliness so his glasses do not become shorthand for always being serious.
Useful tension
He can overprepare while Chaiti has already started. His contribution becomes valuable when he names one specific detail they can test.
Continuity
Square face, round glasses, short wavy hair, red short kurta, blue pocket on his anatomical left, ivory cropped trousers and sandals.
Writing guardrail
No animal-language power. His helpfulness comes from observable evidence. An exact age, district and relationship to Chaiti have not been fixed.

The practical experimenter

Chaiti the practical experimenter

Chaiti wants to turn an interesting idea into something friends can try together. Her strength is practical imagination. Her first success can make her overlook a consequence, so her growth is to own a mistake, revise it and keep her curiosity. A pause after a confident claim lets her laugh at her own correction.

Her visual identity is an oval face and short wavy bob, with a blue A-line tunic, narrow gold neckline and muted-gold cropped trousers. Trying and rethinking are equally important poses. She must not become a permanently correct girl or Nilu’s assistant; either child can discover the answer, need help or make the final joke. [4, 5]

Performance range
Use a ready-to-try posture, delight at a first result, then a pause and a revised action. Let her laugh at her own confident prediction.
Useful tension
She can be right about the method but miss a companion’s need. Nilu may notice that detail, and she can be the one to improve the arrangement.
Continuity
Oval face, short wavy bob, blue A-line tunic with a narrow gold neckline, muted-gold cropped trousers.
Writing guardrail
Rotate who discovers, leads and needs help. Practical competence should not make her an infallible teacher or a constant helper to Nilu.

The eager bird

The eager bird

This companion is a small brown bird with a short tail and no crest. It is a new visual identity, with no fixed personal name in the current records. Alertness and initiative give it a reason to enter a scene; excitement can make a small discovery seem enormous. Proud intent, a sleepy blink and an inconvenient perch create gentle comedy. Keep its movement and body language birdlike. Its response is observable behaviour, not a supernatural translation device. [4, 5]

Performance range
Alert head turns, a brief hop, an oversized reaction to a tiny discovery and an inconveniently timed rest keep the performance readable.
Useful tension
The children’s idea of participation may differ from the bird’s preferred spot or pace. Its needs can change the shape of the solution.
Continuity
A small brown bird, short tail and no crest. Use selected model v2. A personal name and exact species remain open.
Writing guardrail
Body language and chirps communicate behaviour; humans may interpret cautiously but do not translate it as factual speech. Keep the animal ordinary.

The gentle puppy

The gentle puppy

The russet puppy has ordinary anatomy, a cream chest patch and no fixed personal name. It wants to join on its own terms. Patient attention and persistence are strengths; trying alone for a moment too long, or copying before understanding, create small difficulties. It can accept help while keeping the choice to approach, pause or move away. A serious inspection and a final paw adjustment provide its quiet comic rhythm. [4, 5]

Performance range
A serious inspection, a patient wait, a tentative approach and one last paw adjustment create a quiet comic rhythm.
Useful tension
It may try alone slightly too long or imitate a movement without understanding the game. Help works best as an invitation the puppy can accept.
Continuity
Russet coat, cream chest patch and ordinary puppy anatomy. Use selected model v2; a personal name has not yet been chosen.
Writing guardrail
Do not add Elin’s unusual features or history. Give the puppy an active choice in the ending rather than making it a prop to be rescued.

relationships

How the group works

Nilu ↔ Chaiti
Observation and experimentation are complementary. A story works when a careful question changes an attempt, or an attempt gives the observer something new to notice.
Children ↔ eager bird
The bird brings a small need or interruption into a human plan. The children adjust through attention and action, rather than through a magical conversation.
Children ↔ gentle puppy
Care leaves a choice open. A changed seat, accessible bowl or invitation can make participation possible without deciding the puppy’s response for it.
Bird ↔ puppy
A quick alert movement can contrast with a patient inspection. A tiny final animal action is often enough to finish the joke after the human dialogue ends.

The Adventure Book

The 12-page Odia–English Adventure Book extends the cast into activities. Its intended audience is roughly ages 6–9, with an adult helping when needed. Eight activity pages invite observation, drawing, route-finding, rhythm, experimentation, patterns, inclusion, listening and storytelling. These are design intentions; learning outcomes have not been tested. [7]

design

Visual direction & language

Visual identity
Laterite red, sea blue and ripe-paddy gold connect the palette. Recognisable faces, compact silhouettes and ordinary animal anatomy make the cast usable across a comic panel, bookmark and small sticker. Names remain editable outside illustration pixels.
Story tone
Warm, curious and practical. An everyday inconvenience creates enough drama: somewhere to sit, a game, a sound or something to make. Kindness belongs in the solution rather than in a final lesson speech.
Odia and English
Pair short conversational text with visible action. Preserve the same joke, turn-taking and emotional beat in each edition rather than forcing matching line lengths. The activity instructions also need to work when an adult reads them aloud.
Regional connection
Build the setting through Odia, everyday objects and researched craft references as needed. The present brief does not fix a district or represent a named community. A precise future setting should be chosen for the stories it enables.

possibilities

Where this world could go

New editorial proposals for discussion. These are story possibilities, not completed scripts, approved canon or new illustrations.

The paper that would not stand
Nilu wants a neat plan; Chaiti tries a fold. A small test changes both their ideas. This could connect naturally to the existing paper-bridge activity, with adult help where appropriate.
Whose shadow?
A familiar shape changes as the group moves. The bird notices first, the children test their explanations and the puppy supplies the final visual joke. This would explore observation without supernatural explanation.
A quieter rhythm
An enthusiastic pattern becomes too much for one companion. A pause becomes part of the game. The emotional movement is from “everyone copies” to “everyone can take part”.
