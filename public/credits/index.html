Credits & how we work

ଲେଖାରୁ ପ୍ରକାଶନ

From a contribution to an edition

  1. Read the original and identify the author. We preserve the complete contribution and connect it to the writer’s central profile. New profiles are researched carefully, with reliable sources for identity, works and background. Uncertain details remain open for confirmation.
  2. Develop an illustration from the work. The scene grows from the article’s meaning, mood and cultural setting. Cartoon Natural and Poetic Natural offer different ways to serve the work. Review considers story relevance, anatomy and Odisha details.
  3. Bring the edition together. The contributions inform the reading sequence, cover picture and editorial draft. The cover keeps the magazine’s name readable; the editorial draws on the actual writing, with the responsible human editor’s judgment and approval.
  4. Review the reading experience. We check text, author links, images and navigation together, recording corrections and unfinished work. Publication follows the authorization for the edition or book.

The homepage presents all current-issue articles in a randomized order before the other content, while the edition contents and book readers keep their intentional reading sequence.

ବହି ପ୍ରସ୍ତୁତି

Books and source preservation

Books retain their original pages, chapter or poem order, and credits. Extracted text is compared with the scans; uncertain readings remain for editorial review. New illustrations and reading tools support the book without silently replacing its wording or original artwork. Web and downloadable editions each need checks for page order, typography and navigation.

ଅନୁବାଦ ବିଷୟରେ

Human Natural and Poetic Natural translation

We translate from the approved original, asking what the writing does as well as what it says. Human Natural translation carries meaning, cultural context and voice. Poetic Natural also attends to cadence, imagery, silence and the emotional turn, without making every poet sound the same.

Each target language needs its own care. Author explanations and fluent readers’ corrections help us question plausible phrases that miss the intention. AI review supports human literary judgment; it is not a claim of human approval. Credited human translations retain their attribution.

ଚିତ୍ର ବିଷୟରେ

ପତ୍ରିକାର ନୂତନ ଡିଜିଟାଲ୍ ମଲାଟ ଓ ଅନେକ ଗଳ୍ପଚିତ୍ର ତିଆରିରେ କୃତ୍ରିମ ବୁଦ୍ଧିମତ୍ତା (AI) ଉପକରଣ ବ୍ୟବହାର କରାଯାଇଛି। ଚିତ୍ରର ବିଷୟ, ଶୈଳୀ ଓ ସଂଶୋଧନ ମଣିଷଙ୍କ ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦେଶନାରେ ହୁଏ।

New digital cover artwork and many story illustrations are AI-generated, with human creative direction, selection and revision. This disclosure applies across the magazine, including its archive and illustrated digital books. AI also assists with research, additional translations and website development. Its output can contain errors and needs appropriate review.

Generated illustrations are interpretations, not documentary photographs. Poetic imagery can suggest a feeling rather than depict every word. Author portraits require identity-backed references; we do not invent a writer’s likeness. This central explanation accompanies the original creators’ credits throughout the magazine.

AI ସହଯୋଗୀ ଦଳ

ସୃଜନଶୀଳ ସମ୍ପାଦକ ଅହିମାଣିକ୍ୟ ଶତପଥୀଙ୍କ ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦେଶନାରେ AI ଏଜେଣ୍ଟମାନେ କାମ କରିଛନ୍ତି।

Anvesha Acharya — Literary support and archives

Samanta Chandrasekhar — Design and engineering

Drishti Senapati — Quality and usability review

ପ୍ରତି ପ୍ରକାଶନରୁ ଶିଖିବା

Learning with each publication

We retain editor-approved corrections, useful examples and their context, preserving earlier versions. A choice for one poem is not a rule for every poet. Changes to an original prompt a fresh look at its translations and images. This grows our editorial knowledge; it does not mean we have trained a new AI model.

What people teach AI about a language

A personal reflection by Ahimanikya Satapathy

I think of AI as an intern in a company: capable, quick to contribute, and still in need of guidance. It makes mistakes. A confident answer can miss something that a person with lived experience notices immediately. Yet I believe that patient feedback can make AI a powerful creative partner, with possibilities beyond what we can imagine today.

Language is more than the rules written in a grammar book. It carries a community’s culture: how people express affection, tease one another, show respect, grieve, protest and leave things unsaid. A sentence can be grammatically correct and still feel wrong to the people whose language it is. Helping AI recognize that difference takes human experience and judgment.

For Sishu Kishore, that attention begins with Odia and extends to the languages in which a work is translated. When I say I am teaching the AI I work with, I mean explaining cultural context, questioning its choices, comparing versions and keeping the lessons from each correction in the translation process. Each revision is an opportunity to carry the work’s meaning, emotional force and voice more faithfully.

My knowledge of Odia is one part of that work. Readers fluent in each target language bring knowledge I cannot supply alone. Their feedback matters: where a phrase feels unnatural, where an image loses its meaning, where a poem no longer sounds like a person speaking. That collaboration is where I see AI becoming a powerful creative partner.

ମୂଳ ଲେଖା ଓ କଳାକୃତି

ଗଳ୍ପ, କବିତା ଓ ଅନ୍ୟ ଲେଖାର ଶ୍ରେୟ ସମ୍ପୃକ୍ତ ଲେଖକଙ୍କର। ପିଲାଙ୍କ ଚିତ୍ର, ମୂଳ ମୁଦ୍ରିତ ମଲାଟ ଓ ସ୍କାନ୍ କରାଯାଇଥିବା ବହିର ଚିତ୍ର ସେମାନଙ୍କ ମୂଳ ସ୍ରଷ୍ଟାଙ୍କ କୃତି। ସେଗୁଡ଼ିକୁ AI-ନିର୍ମିତ ବୋଲି ଦର୍ଶାଯାଉନାହିଁ।
